# Research Method - Completed Training Dossiers

> **All source organisations, records, people, figures, policy terms and events in this lab are fictional.** This is a closed practice environment. It does not represent the Dutch state, a real regulator, a real insurer or real health coverage.

## A repeatable evidence method

1. Resolve the entity: parent group, operating insurer, brand, policy and service suppliers are different records.
2. Atomise the claim: one proposition per claim record.
3. Record source identity, publisher, URL or local path, publication date, verification date, page/section, source type and limitations.
4. Distinguish company assertion, primary record, independent reporting, regulatory-style record, provider-side evidence and user testimony.
5. Set evidence status and confidence separately.
6. Trace both support and contradiction. Never hide conflicts by choosing the tidier source.
7. Keep legal entitlement, contractual entitlement and practical accessibility separate.
8. For care access, require a named provider, site, treatment, service code, referral, contract period, capacity and wait-time evidence.
9. For customer metrics, record numerator, denominator, population, exclusions, period and channel.
10. Preserve unknowns. Do not calculate an overall winner from incomparable or incomplete evidence.

## Company experiences are intentionally different

- **Noordlicht:** desktop archaeology. Explore visual brand layers, member governance records, supplier schedules and archive snapshots.
- **Rivierzorg:** systems investigation. Follow event codes, API fields, queues, service-level definitions and network data.
- **Kompas:** household and cooperative research. Separate insured lives, household eligibility, member status, voting rights and regional service boundaries.

The research method overlaps, but the discovery interface and evidence types vary. This models the fact that a good researcher adapts source-finding tactics to an organisation rather than repeating one identical checklist.

## Fictional institutions and external sources

The KRO, DBR, NZT, Noorder Cooperative Bank and De Zorglijn are invented institutions and publications. They are labelled as simulations throughout the files. Their names intentionally evoke the functions of public registers, tax filing services, financial oversight, banks and specialist journalism without impersonating real institutions.

## Expanded cross-company case: unresolved infrastructure hypothesis

The shared case adds three simulated archive exhibits (NLX-2026-01, RVX-2026-02 and KPX-2026-03). They are intentionally incomplete leads, not proof of a conspiracy. Keep ordinary consolidation, quiet common infrastructure, fragmented systems with a common vendor, and changing arrangements as competing explanations. No supplied source proves coordinated intent, a single hidden controller or harm to members. A strong investigation may conclude that selected supplier, capital and workflow relationships exist while the full structure and practical effects remain unknown.
