CORPORATE ARCHIVE / 2004–2027

A history of making things happen.

Milestones assembled from company filings, product notes, customer announcements and internal records.

Timeline

  1. 2004 — Civic Forms Pilot. A consortium of municipal IT contractors prototypes a shared intake form library.
  2. 2006 — Stichting GoedGeregeld. The pilot is transferred into a foundation with a mandate to standardise public-facing forms.
  3. 2008 — First insurer deployment. A claims intake module is adapted for a regional health administrator.
  4. 2010 — BV conversion. Product development moves to GoedGeregeld Software B.V.; the foundation retains a minority golden-share provision.
  5. 2012 — Workflow 2.0. Queue, task and document modules are bundled under the SamenWerkt suite.
  6. 2014 — Noordlicht framework. A multi-year enterprise agreement adds identity matching and document intake.
  7. 2015 — Municipal cloud migration. Hosting shifts to a Dutch data-centre consortium; subcontractor schedules become customer-specific.
  8. 2017 — Rivierzorg rollout. Regional partner routing is configured through a white-label portal.
  9. 2018 — Atlas Capital investment. Growth capital funds a reseller network and a new analytics product.
  10. 2019 — Service catalogue merger. Three legacy ticket systems are consolidated into a single customer portal.
  11. 2020 — Remote Work Pack. Video meetings, document rooms and task boards are sold as a single “continuity environment”.
  12. 2021 — Kompas declines deployment. The cooperative's procurement committee records that it will retain its own open-source case-management stack.
  13. 2022 — OneDesk acquisition. GoedGeregeld acquires a small workflow integrator; historic customer identifiers are mapped but not fully harmonised.
  14. 2023 — Metric alignment programme. Customer dashboards adopt a common completion vocabulary; customer-level definitions remain configurable.
  15. 2024 — GGD 6.0 launch. A new low-code interface and “single pane” reporting layer are introduced.
  16. 2025 — Service continuity review. An external review identifies incomplete subcontractor schedules across several legacy deployments.
  17. 2026 — Horizon consolidation. Product teams propose a unified customer identity graph; the board postpones a group-wide rollout.
  18. 2027 — Platform federation. Customers retain separate tenants while sharing selected modules, support channels and update infrastructure.
  19. 2027 — Assurance catalogue. The company publishes a standard evidence export pack covering configuration snapshots, workflow events and customer-specific exclusions.

Ownership and influence

Share register summaries identify Atlas Civic Growth Fund II (34%), Stichting GoedGeregeld (12%), employee participation vehicles (9%) and dispersed investors (45%). The foundation's special consent rights are described differently across the 2010 articles and 2024 governance summary. No single public document in this archive resolves every reserved matter.

Historical executives and investor representatives appear in board minutes, but attendance is not evidence of control over customer decisions.
