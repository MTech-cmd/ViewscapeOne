Part F — The quiet infrastructure

Across the three companies, the supplied records point to overlapping administrative infrastructure: Kade Data & Dossier B.V. appears in different service contexts; Wetering Civic Capital B.V. appears in a shareholder record and a financing summary; and public reporting suggests that common back-office arrangements may exist beyond the visible contracts.

Do not promote a hypothesis into a finding. The records establish selected legal, service and financing relationships. They do not establish a single command structure, a coordinated plan to deny care, or that every company knew about every other arrangement.
Thread 01 / The same supplier?Compare the named legal entity, service scope, dates, subcontractors and contracting party. A common supplier does not prove common control.
Thread 02 / The capital trailWetering appears in a financing summary and corporate filing. The bank summary does not name its customers; the filing does not explain operational influence.
Thread 03 / The percentage problem82/89 hospital organisations, active hospital addresses, and organisations with any active agreement are not equivalent denominators. Their divergence may be ordinary methodology—or conceal important operational differences.
Thread 04 / The missing scheduleMultiple records refer to annexes, exception routes or processor schedules that are not supplied. Their absence is a research limitation, not proof of what they contain.

Part G — Competing explanations

Maintain at least three live models. Update confidence only when evidence changes.

Model A / Ordinary consolidationShared suppliers and financing reflect routine procurement, scale and sector-wide outsourcing. The differences are explained by separate contracts and metrics.
Model B / Quiet common infrastructureA small number of service and capital entities may connect otherwise separate brands. The operational reach and governance implications remain unclear.
Model C / Fragmented systems with a common vendorSimilar names and workflows may create an impression of coordination while the actual systems, decisions and responsibilities remain fragmented.
Model D / Mixed or changing arrangementRelationships may have changed over time; snapshots from different periods may look more coherent than the underlying history.

Part H — The case has a hard ceiling

This investigation is intentionally not fully solvable from the supplied corpus. Several decisive records are missing, redacted, scoped narrowly or held by parties outside the visible record. New clues may sharpen the map while creating new conflicts in chronology or legal scope.

  • No complete, signed master services agreement covering all three companies is supplied.
  • No complete subcontractor register or end-to-end data-flow map is supplied.
  • The bank's public summary does not identify its customers or establish how financing affected operational decisions.
  • Registry extracts establish filed identities and selected relationships, not informal control or day-to-day authority.
  • No patient-level linked dataset establishes whether any shared arrangement changed actual care access.
  • No source establishes a coordinated conspiracy, intent to harm members, or a single hidden decision-maker.

Your final deliverable is not “who is behind it?” It is a defensible map of what is established, what is plausible, what is contradicted, what remains unknown, and which exact records could discriminate between the competing models.
