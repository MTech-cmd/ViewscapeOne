# Dutch Health Insurance Picker 2027

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Initial foundation and technical prototype  
**Insurance year:** 2027

## Project definition

An independent project to help people understand, research and compare Dutch health insurance options for the 2027 insurance year. It considers three separate dimensions:

1. **Healthcare suitability:** how well an option accommodates healthcare needs.
2. **Consumer suitability:** practical policy conditions, services and restrictions.
3. **Corporate responsibility:** group-level performance concerning climate, human rights, labour practices and transparency.

The application informs decisions. It does not make decisions on behalf of users.

## Why it exists

Contractual coverage alone does not establish practical access. Contracted providers, regional availability, waiting times, referrals, reimbursement restrictions and financial conditions can affect access. Corporate ownership, customer-facing providers and individual contracts must therefore be distinguished.

## Core entities

- **Insurance groups:** corporate organisations and group-level responsibility.
- **Insurance providers:** customer-facing providers/brands and provider-level consumer experience and accessibility.
- **Individual policies:** specific insurance contracts for a particular insurance year.
- **Claims and Sources:** evidence findings and the material supporting them.

Relationship: one group may have multiple providers; one provider may offer multiple policies. Corporate assessments are not duplicated across providers, and group affiliation does not establish identical consumer outcomes.

## Evidence principles

Record source title, publisher, direct URL, publication date, verification date, applicable entity, evidence summary, limitations, confidence and conflicting information. Unknown remains unknown. A missing source is not evidence of poor performance; a corporate promise is not proof of implementation.

## Principles

1. Evidence before conclusions.
2. Transparency over unexplained scores.
3. Unknown values remain unknown.
4. Corporate, provider and policy assessments remain separate.
5. Consumer suitability and institutional responsibility remain independently visible.
6. Users control their priorities.
7. Important decisions remain under Operator authority.
8. Significant changes are documented.
9. Entities improve through feedback and learning.
10. Automated assessments never replace human judgement.
